Case study by Bahareh, product designer & builder

Trust is the first thing you lose when you move countries.

Onnest is a two-sided marketplace where newcomers in France get paid help from verified advisors, answers from people who’ve been through it, and AI that points them to the right person. I designed it, made every product decision, and took it from prototype to production in about two weeks.

Where this started

When I moved from Iran to France, nothing about my past could be checked.

Iran is cut off from the international banking system. My salary history, tax records and bank statements meant nothing to a French bank or a landlord. There was simply no way for them to verify my history. The account only opened because my HR manager came to the bank with me and vouched for me in person, a kind of help most newcomers don’t have.

My friends hit the same wall with rentals, banks and the prefecture. Capable, employed people, treated as unknowns because their paperwork came from the wrong country.

The insight

Newcomers don’t lack information. The internet is full of guides. What they lack is someone trusted to stand next to them: a person who knows the system, is accountable for their answer, and speaks their language. Onnest is designed to be that person.

The problem

Every newcomer faces several walls at once, each in its own language.

The help that exists is scattered. Facebook groups give free but conflicting answers. Lawyers are expensive and hard to vet. Nothing covers everything in one place, with people you can trust, at a price that fits a quick question.

Banking

No verifiable history

Foreign payslips and statements can’t be checked, so opening an account depends on luck or a sponsor.

Housing

No dossier landlords accept

Rentals require French guarantors, French payslips and French tax notices that a newcomer doesn’t have yet.

Immigration

No margin for error

One missing document at the prefecture can mean months of delay and a job put at risk.

Early feedback

I put the prototype in front of newcomers before building anything real.

I posted on Telegram that I was building an app to help immigrants in France and asked for people to try it and tell me what they thought. Five friends tested the Figma Make prototype, casually and in their own time. They found it interesting and helpful.

Their reaction changed the scope. It made me think the product could help far more than Iranians, so I added two new communities, for Arab and Indian newcomers, who face the same wall in a different language.

It was a small, informal test, so I treat it as an early signal and not as proof. The next round is with real advisors and paying users.

How it works as a business

Three layers, each with one job.

Free content brings people in, AI turns a vague worry into a specific need, and advisors turn that need into revenue.

Acquisition & retention

Community

Language-based rooms, community Q&A and practical guides. Free to use, so newcomers arrive through search and word of mouth and come back between paid sessions. Serious questions are pointed to verified advisors.

4 rooms, 5 languagesIranian, Arab, Indian and Business communities

Conversion

AI Smart Match

A newcomer describes their situation in their own words. A short guided conversation captures category, urgency, language and budget, then returns ranked advisors with a reason for each match.

The product

From “I don’t know who to ask” to a resolved question.

1. Find

Describe the problem, not the category.

Newcomers rarely know whether their problem is “admin” or “immigration.” Smart Match lets them start in their own words, then explains every recommendation, for example: speaks Persian and specializes in permit renewals. The reason is what makes the match credible.

2. Book

A fixed price, and your money is protected.

Every advisor profile shows response time, languages and availability before the price. Checkout explains exactly where the money goes: held until you’re helped, refundable within 48 hours.

3. Get help

A written thread you can come back to.

Each request becomes a chat thread with document sharing, so a newcomer can send a residence permit or a payslip and keep the answer. The Requests page shows every engagement by status.

4. Belong

A room that speaks your language.

Communities are organized by origin and language, with full right-to-left support for Farsi and Arabic. Clear guidelines and AI moderation keep them useful, and a “not legal advice” rule points serious questions to verified advisors.

Key decisions

Each decision gave something up on purpose.

I kept every product call in a written decisions log. These six shaped the product most.

Chat only. Calls cut from the first version.

The prototype had voice, video and in-person options. I cut all three; they now show as “Coming soon.” Async chat with a document thread fits the actual job: send your permit, get a written answer you can re-read.

Gave upLive conversation, which some users prefer
GotA written record, flexible hours for advisors, and the biggest technical risk removed

Payments built, but switched off.

Charging before advisors are proven to show up would kill trust on day one. The beta is free, but every engagement already records its price and payment state, and all bookings pass through one checkout function.

Gave upRevenue during the beta
GotTrust and liquidity first. Turning payments on is one setting, with zero rework.

Four stages instead of three.

The engineering draft used Waiting, Active, Closed. I changed it to Upcoming, Waiting, Active, Resolved. A booking for Thursday is a different state from one waiting right now, and “Resolved” tells the user the outcome where “Closed” only describes the system.

Gave upA simpler status model
GotStatuses that match what users actually expect to happen next

Only the advisor resolves, and nothing reopens.

A follow-up question creates a new engagement through “Book a follow-up.” Each payment stays tied to one clear outcome, which is what refunds and reviews depend on.

Gave upThe convenience of reopening a thread
GotClean records for escrow, refunds and ratings

Advisors sign up themselves, but stay invisible until verified.

Self-serve onboarding lets supply grow without me in the loop. Admin approval protects the one promise the product makes: the “Verified” badge means something.

Gave upInstant advisor listings
GotScalable onboarding without diluting trust

Data stays in Paris.

Users share passports, residence permits and payslips. All data lives in the EU (Paris region), every table is locked so each person sees only their own records, and documents are shared through expiring links.

Gave upSome flexibility in hosting choices
GotGDPR compliance from the first table, not as a later fix

Designing the side nobody drew

I designed the advisor app by mirroring the newcomer’s.

The prototype covered only newcomers. Instead of inventing the advisor experience from scratch, I derived it: every action a newcomer takes implies an action someone on the other side must be able to take.

When a newcomer…The advisor or admin must be able to…
Sends a request→Receive it in an inbox grouped by the same four statuses
Sees “Waiting for advisor to join”→End the wait: their first reply turns the request Active
Uploads a document→View and review it inside the thread
Sees “Resolved by advisor”→Resolve the thread, which makes it read-only
Sees a “Verified” badge→Get verified by an admin before appearing anywhere
Pays a fixed price→Set their price and connect a payout account

The result is an advisor app that matches the newcomer’s mental model one to one, built on the same design system, so it looks like Onnest even though it was never drawn in Figma.

Trust, made visible

Trust can’t be a tagline, so I designed it into specific moments.

Every match explains itself

A recommendation without a reason is a guess. Each match states why this advisor fits this person.

Money is held, not taken

Funds are released only when the question is resolved, with a 48-hour refund window.

Verified means verified

No advisor appears anywhere until an admin approves them. Everything stays in-app, with no contact sharing.

Rules before rooms

Each community opens with clear guidelines, AI moderation and an honest “not legal advice” line.

The rules I set before any code

  1. Plan before codeNo app code until I approved a full plan: screens, data model and milestones.
  2. One milestone at a timeBuild one, then stop so I can verify it against the prototype.
  3. No silent product decisionsReal choices come back to me as options with a recommendation.
  4. Shared memory in writingA decisions log, a plan and a status file kept every session in sync.

Results

A live product, not a prototype.

The full journey works end to end on a real database: match, book, chat with document sharing, and resolve, mirrored on the advisor side.

Not built yet, stated openly

  • Community content is still seed data.
  • Document upload has the database and security in place, but no interface yet.
  • Google and Apple sign-in are built and waiting on provider credentials.

What’s next

  • Turn payments on once there are enough active advisors.
  • Add voice and video calls as a premium option.
  • Wrap both apps for the App Store and Google Play.
  • Build an admin dashboard for verification and moderation.

Reflection

Writing the code was the fast part. Deciding was the slow part.

What to cut, what to delay, and what one word, “Resolved” or “Closed,” promises to someone who is already anxious. AI made building cheap, which made product judgment the whole job.
